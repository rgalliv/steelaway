# Scope Boundaries

The allocations that generate most steel scope disputes. Each is a *question to
resolve in writing*, not a rule — the answer is project-specific and lives in the
contract documents.

## The recurring boundaries

| Boundary | The question |
|---|---|
| **Miscellaneous metals** | Which items sit outside the structural package — lintels, embeds, ladders, bollards, supports? |
| **Open web joists / girders** | Supplied, erected, or both — and by whom? |
| **Metal deck** | Furnish, install, or install-only? Who does deck welding and side-lap fastening? |
| **Shear studs** | Shop or field applied? Who inspects them? |
| **Embeds and anchor rods** | Who furnishes, who sets, who verifies placement, and what tolerance governs? |
| **Base plates and grouting** | Who furnishes plates, who grouts, who sets and verifies elevation? |
| **Connection design** | Prescribed on drawings, or delegated to the fabricator? If delegated, who seals it and who reviews? |
| **Field welding vs bolting** | What is permitted where, and who supplies inspection? |
| **Surface prep and coatings** | Shop primer, field touch-up, galvanizing, intumescent, sprayed fireproofing — and who preps the surface for the next coat? |
| **Fireproofing prep** | Does primer compatibility with the fireproofing get verified, and by whom? |
| **Stairs, railings, grating** | Structural scope or misc metals? Design responsibility? |
| **Temporary bracing** | Who designs, furnishes, installs, and removes — and when? |
| **Erection aids** | Left in place or removed? Who pays for removal and repair? |
| **Survey and layout** | Who establishes control, who verifies as-built position? |
| **Special inspection** | Owner-furnished or contractor-furnished, and what does a rejection cost whom? |

## How to use this

Two moments:

1. **Bidding.** Walk the list against the RFP. Anything the documents do not
   clearly assign becomes an exclusion or a pre-bid RFI. Silence is not
   agreement — it is a future argument.

2. **Post-award.** Walk it again against the executed contract. What you excluded
   at bid does not always survive into the agreement, and the delta is unpriced
   risk you now own.

## The default-division trap

The AISC Code of Standard Practice sets default divisions of work for many of
these, and contracts routinely modify them. Two failure modes:

- Assuming the AISC default governs when the contract has overridden it.
- Assuming industry custom settles something the documents leave silent.

Both lose. Where the documents are silent, the item is **ambiguous** — get it in
writing early, while the answer is still cheap.
